Precise measurement of the CKM angle 𝜸\gamma with a novel approach

BESIII and LHCb collaborations††
          †
          
          
          
        Authors are listed at the end of this Letter.

      
A measurement of the CKM angle γ\gamma is performed by applying a novel, unbinned, model-independent approach to datasets of electron-positron collisions collected by the BESIII experiment and proton-proton collisions by the LHCb experiment,
corresponding to integrated luminosities of 8 fb−1\text{\,fb}^{-1} and 9 fb−1\text{\,fb}^{-1}, respectively.
The C​PC\!P-violating phase γ\gamma is determined from B±→D(→KS0h′⁣+h′⁣−)h±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D(\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-})h^{\pm} decays in LHCb data, where h(′)h^{(\prime)} is either a pion or kaon,
while the corresponding strong-phase parameters are measured using doubly tagged D→KS/L0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−{D\rightarrow K_{\rm S/L}^{0}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-}} decays in the quantum-correlated D​D¯D{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}} system present in BESIII data.
A joint fit to both datasets, which allows for a simultaneous determination of the associated C​PC\!P-violating observables and strong-phase parameters, yields γ=(71.3±5.0)∘{\gamma=(71.3\pm 5.0)^{\circ}}.
The result is the most precise to date and consistent with previous measurements and world averages.

Violation of symmetry under charge-parity (C​PC\!P) transformation
is a necessary ingredient to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry of the Universe [1]. In the Standard Model of particle physics (SM), C​PC\!P violation is explained by a complex phase in the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix [2, *Kobayashi:1973fv], which governs quark flavor mixing in the weak interaction.
The unitarity of this matrix leads to relationships that can be visualized as triangles in the complex plane, known as Unitarity Triangles.
Among their internal angles, γ=ϕ3≡arg(−Vu​dVu​b∗/Vc​dVc​b∗){\gamma=\phi_{3}\equiv\arg(-{V_{{u}{d}}^{\phantom{\ast}}}{V_{{u}{b}}^{\ast}}/{V_{{c}{d}}^{\phantom{\ast}}}{V_{{c}{b}}^{\ast}})}, with Vx​yV_{xy} representing the transition amplitude from quark xx to quark yy, is of particular interest.
It can be determined directly either through the interference between b→c​u¯​sb\!\rightarrow c{\overline{{u}}}s and b→u​c¯​sb\!\rightarrow u{\overline{{c}}}s transitions at leading order (tree-level), e.g. B±→D​K±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D{{K}^{\pm}} decays, with negligible theoretical uncertainties [4, 5], or in charmless BB-meson decays [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12].
In contrast, indirect measurements of γ\gamma often involve loop-mediated processes, which are potentially sensitive to physics beyond the SM.
Comparisons between direct tree-level measurements and indirect measurements provide a powerful test of CKM unitarity and could reveal new sources of C​PC\!P violation.
Currently, the precision of the direct measurement [13] is about a factor of 33 worse than those using indirect constraints [14, 15, 16] assuming CKM unitarity.
Consequently, improving the precision of the direct measurements of γ\gamma is a crucial goal in particle physics.

Direct measurements of γ\gamma are primarily obtained from B→D(∗)​K(∗)B\!\rightarrow D^{(*)}K^{(*)} decays,
where the global precision is currently dominated by the LHCb experiment, which has recently determined γ=(62.8±2.6)∘{\gamma=(62.8\pm 2.6){{}^{\circ}}} [13].
The most precise single measurement to date comes from the analysis of B±→D​h±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow Dh^{\pm} decays, and D→KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} decay with h(′)h^{(\prime)} being a kaon or pion, where DD denotes a coherent superposition of D0{{D}^{0}} and D¯0{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}{}^{0}} mesons.
The decay phase-space amplitudes can be described in terms of the Dalitz-plot [17, 18] variables,
defined here as the KS0​h′⁣+{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+} and KS0​h′⁣−{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime-} squared masses.
The measurement exploits strong-phase variation across the Dalitz plot using a binned phase-space approach [19, 20, 21], yielding γ=(68.7−5.1+5.2)∘{\gamma=(68.7^{+5.2}_{-5.1}){{}^{\circ}}} [22].
To determine γ\gamma from B±→D​h±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow Dh^{\pm} data,
information of the strong-phase difference between D0{{D}^{0}} and D¯0{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}{}^{0}} decays to KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−{{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-}} is required,
which has been measured independently in quantum-correlated D​D¯D{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}} decays produced in e+​e−{e^{+}e^{-}} collisions by the CLEO [23] and BESIII experiments [24, 25, 26].
The binned technique provides an unbiased determination of γ\gamma by avoiding model-dependent assumptions.
However, it sacrifices some statistical sensitivity because events within each bin are averaged for strong-phase values and decay densities, thereby neglecting intra-bin information.
Current binning schemes are estimated to retain approximately 85% of the sensitivity to γ\gamma [23].
Advanced analysis techniques that increase sensitivity while preserving model independence are therefore essential for precision tests of CKM unitarity.

In this Letter, a new measurement of γ\gamma using a novel set of weight functions based on data from the BESIII and LHCb collaborations is presented.
An extensive description of the experimental procedure along with a comprehensive collection of results can be found in a companion article [28].
The method employs an unbinned approach that combines two components to maximize sensitivity.
The first component, following the concept introduced in Ref. [27],
utilizes a Fourier expansion of the strong-phase difference ϕ⁡(𝐳)\mathbf{\phi}(\mathbf{z}), to capture its variation across the Dalitz plot,
and is calculated from amplitude models of D→KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} decays [29, 30, 31]. The second component introduced here, wopt​(𝐳)w^{\rm opt}(\mathbf{z}), is the optimal weight incorporating sensitivity changes due to rD​(𝐳)r_{D}(\mathbf{z}) and experimental effects such as efficiency and background distributions over the Dalitz plot.
The values of woptw^{\rm opt} for the datasets, shown in Fig. 1, enhance statistical sensitivity by assigning greater importance to regions that exhibit larger C​PC\!P-violating effects.
While amplitude models are used to construct the weight functions, the γ\gamma measurement remains model independent as the models only define integration weights without directly parameterizing the data.
Improved amplitude models may lead to sensitivity gains through more accurate weights, and this approach could therefore benefit from future amplitude studies with larger data samples.

The BESIII dataset provides the dominant constraint on the strong-phase parameters. It was collected at the ψ⁡(3770){\psi(3770)} resonance in e+​e−{e^{+}e^{-}} collisions, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 8 fb−1\text{\,fb}^{-1}.
Meanwhile, C​PC\!P-violating parameters are determined from LHCb datasets that were collected during Run 1 and Run 2 in p​ppp collisions at center-of-mass energies s=7,8{\sqrt{s}=7,8} and 13 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9 fb−1\text{\,fb}^{-1}. The LHCb data also provide marginal sensitivity to the strong-phase parameters through the joint analysis [28].

The BESIII detector [33] records symmetric e+​e−e^{+}e^{-} collisions
provided by the BEPCII storage ring [34] at s=3.773​ GeV\sqrt{s}=3.773\text{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}. Detection efficiencies and backgrounds are estimated by simulation samples produced with a Geant4-based [35] software package. The simulation models the beam energy spread and initial-state radiation in e+​e−e^{+}e^{-} annihilations with the generator kkmc [36, 37].
The LHCb detector is a
single-arm forward spectrometer covering the pseudorapidity range
2<η<52<\eta<5,
described in detail in Refs. [38, 39].
Simulation is required to model and correct the efficiencies in LHCb data.
In the simulation, p​ppp collisions are generated using
Pythia [40, *Sjostrand:2006za]
with a specific LHCb configuration [42].
Decays of unstable particles
are described by EvtGen [43], in which final-state
radiation is generated using Photos [44].
The interaction of the generated particles with the detector, and its response,
are implemented using the Geant4 toolkit [45, 35] as described in
Ref. [46].
Details on the simulation samples generated for this analysis can be found in the companion article [28].

In the BESIII dataset, quantum-correlated D​D¯D{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}} decays are selected where one DD meson is reconstructed in the D→KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} or D→KL0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} final state, and the accompanying D¯{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}} meson is selected in a tag mode which can be classified into three categories summarized in Table 1. Selections of the final-state particles in the doubly tagged decays are identical to those in Ref. [47], where the binned strong-phase parameters in the D→KS/L0​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow K^{0}_{\mathrm{S/L}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} decays are measured using the same dataset.
Following the method presented in Ref. [47], the selected final-state particles are combined to form the doubly tagged KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−{{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-}} and KL0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−{{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-}} candidates.

Possible sources of background are estimated through simulated samples of generic DD decays. The dominant peaking backgrounds are D→π+​π−​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} for D→KS0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} decays and D→KS0(→π0π0)h′⁣+h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}(\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0})h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} for D→KL0​h′⁣+​h′⁣−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{L}}}h^{\prime+}h^{\prime-} decays.
The DD-decay observables, as listed in Table 1, are measured with the selected signal D​D¯D{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}} candidates.
To consider the background contributions in these observables, the weight functions in Eq. S0.EGx1 are applied to the simulated background events and the weighted background events are subtracted from the DD-decay observables.
Efficiencies obtained from BESIII simulation are applied to correct the modification of the Dalitz-plot distributions due to reconstruction and selection effects.

In the LHCb dataset, KS0→π+​π−{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}\!\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} decays are reconstructed in either the long category, where both pion tracks leave hits in the vertex detector and subsequent tracking detectors, or the downstream category, where the decay occurs downstream of the vertex detector and tracks are detected in all other tracking detectors.
The KS0{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}} candidates are combined with two tracks assigned with either the pion or kaon mass hypothesis to form D→KS0​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} and D→KS0​K+​K−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}} candidates, respectively. An additional kaon or pion track is then added to reconstruct B±→D​K±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D{{K}^{\pm}} or B±→D​π±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D{{\pi}^{\pm}} candidates.
Candidate selection and signal extraction follow the same procedure as the measurement using the binned phase-space approach [22].

The signal extraction for BB-candidate decays is based on a fit procedure that is detailed in the companion article [28].
Per-event signal weights are computed with the sPlot technique [48], ensuring the weighted Dalitz-plot distributions represent those of the signal. The signal candidates for B±→D​K±B^{\pm}\!\rightarrow D{{K}^{\pm}} decays are shown in Figs. 1(c) and (d), displaying the Dalitz distributions for D→KS0​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} and D→KS0​K+​K−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}} decays, respectively.
Their signal yields are 13 450±130{13\,450\pm 130} and 2005±50{2005\pm 50}.

Per-event efficiency corrections are applied according to simulated signal decays for the BB-decay observables. The detailed procedures of background subtraction and efficiency correction are described in the companion article [28].

Systematic uncertainties on the measured observables from both experiments are found to be subleading to the statistical uncertainties.
Following the binned measurements of the strong-phase parameters [47] and C​PC\!P-violating parameters [22], the same sources of systematic uncertainty have been considered. Several additional sources are considered in this measurement due to the new analysis method.

For each source of systematic uncertainty from the analysis of BESIII data, its impact on the C​PC\!P observables is studied by performing joint fits to data or pseudoexperiments with the LHCb component unchanged. Uncertainties from the estimation of yields of tag modes, and from background yields and distributions, are determined from pseudoexperiments.
Additional systematic uncertainties are considered, including the biases extracted from pseudoexperiments, statistical fluctuations in the flavor-tag coefficients, inputs of the hadronic parameters for flavor tags and charm-mixing parameters, the amplitude models used to determine the correlation matrices, limited statistics of the phase-space simulation samples for efficiency profiles, limited statistics of the simulation samples for signal efficiencies, and the discrepancy in resolution between data and simulation. The total systematic uncertainty from BESIII accounts for approximately 10% of the statistical uncertainty and is subdominant compared to that from LHCb.

Systematic uncertainties on the C​PC\!P observables from the analysis of LHCb data derive from modifications of the fit model.
Uncertainties related to insignificant background contributions, not included in the BB-candidate mass fits,
and physics effects, such as C​PC\!P violation in partially reconstructed B→D(∗)​K(∗)B\!\rightarrow D^{(*)}K^{(*)} decays, are assessed using pseudoexperiments. These are generated with such effects included, then fitted with the baseline model, where the observed differences are assigned as systematic uncertainties.
Uncertainties from the fixed relative yields of partially reconstructed backgrounds, particle-identification efficiencies and mass-shape parameters are evaluated using a frequentist approach. The parameters are varied within their uncertainties, and the fits to data repeated for each variation. The resulting spread in the measured observables is taken as the systematic uncertainty.
The effects of C​PC\!P violation in KS0{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}} decay, its interactions with the detector, and DD mixing, are neglected in the baseline fit.
Their potential biases are quantified by generating pseudosignal samples that incorporate these effects according to Refs. [51, 52],
and fitting with the baseline model. The difference between the fitted and input values is assigned as a systematic uncertainty.
The magnitudes of the systematic uncertainties from these sources are found to be comparable to those in the binned approach [22].

It is assumed that there is no correlation between the mass-fit variables and Dalitz-plot distributions, as required by the sPlot technique.
Bias from their potential correlation is assessed by investigating Dalitz-plot-dependent mass shapes from simulation.
The discrepancy between LHCb data and simulation is corrected by comparing predicted distributions with those from B±→D​π±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D{{\pi}^{\pm}} data, where the background contribution is negligible.
Detector resolution is taken into account by smearing the Dalitz-plot distributions of pseudosignal according to the differences between the reconstructed and true mKS0​h′⁣±2m^{2}_{{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}h^{\prime\pm}} in simulation.
For each source, pseudoexperiments are generated including each effect and fitted with the baseline model.
The mean biases of the C​PC\!P observables are assigned as systematic uncertainties.
The systematic uncertainty from the simulation sample size is found to be negligible.
Additionally, a systematic uncertainty is assigned to account for the small bias in the fit method, derived from a pseudoexperiment study conducted with the baseline model.
These additional sources are not dominant, hence the total systematic uncertainties remain at a level comparable to that in Ref. [22].

These results are consistent with world averages [14, 53, 16] and the latest combination of LHCb measurements [13], in which the binned phase-space measurement is included.
As current measurements performed with the excited B±→D(∗)​h(∗)±{{B}^{\pm}}\!\rightarrow D^{(*)}h^{(*)\pm}  channels still rely on binned strong-phase inputs, the unbinned results contained in this Letter cannot yet be used in combination to determine a γ\gamma average due to unclear correlations between the two approaches.
Nevertheless, this novel approach provides a complementary determination and establishes the foundation for an improved route to precision measurements of the CKM angle γ\gamma.

To summarize, the most precise single and direct measurement of the CKM angle γ\gamma is achieved by a simultaneous analysis of the datasets of quantum-correlated D​D¯D{{\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptD}}} decays and BB-meson decays.
Higher-order Fourier expansions of the charm strong-phase differences could be exploited to further improve the determination of the γ\gamma angle by taking advantage of the 20 fb−1\text{\,fb}^{-1} of ψ⁡(3770)\psi(3770) data collected by BESIII [54] and the Run 3 dataset of LHCb [55], corresponding to an increase in integrated luminosities by a factor of about 2.5. For LHCb, additional gains are expected from the improved trigger efficiency for hadronic channels in the Run 3 detector.
The novel approach, developed for the decay D→KS0​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}, and extended to the decay D→KS0​K+​K−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}}, demonstrates that leveraging amplitude variations across the phase space with greater precision can enhance sensitivity in future γ\gamma measurements.
Moreover, the unbinned methodologies establish a blueprint for future applications to other multibody DD decays, e.g. D→K+​K−​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{K}^{+}}{{K}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}, D→π+​π−​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} and D→π+​K−​π+​π−D\!\rightarrow{{\pi}^{+}}{{K}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}.
