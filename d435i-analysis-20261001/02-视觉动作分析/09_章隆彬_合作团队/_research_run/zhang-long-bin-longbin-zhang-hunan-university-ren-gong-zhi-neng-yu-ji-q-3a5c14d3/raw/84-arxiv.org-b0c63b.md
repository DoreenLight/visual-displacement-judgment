Abstract

    
Using (2.71±0.01)×109(2.71\pm 0.01)\times 10^{9} ψ⁡(3686)\psi(3686) events collected with the BESIII detector, a joint full angular distribution analysis is carried out for the process ψ(3686)→Ω−(→ΛK−)Ω¯+(→Λ¯K+)\psi(3686)\to\Omega^{-}(\to\Lambda K^{-})\,\bar{\Omega}^{+}(\to\bar{\Lambda}K^{+}). The first simultaneous measurement of the weak decay parameters ϕΩ−\phi_{\Omega^{-}} and ϕΩ¯+\phi_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}} for Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda and Ω¯+→K+​Λ¯\bar{\Omega}^{+}\to K^{+}\bar{\Lambda} is performed, yielding the first result for the CP-sensitive observable, ϕCP=(−0.004±0.055±0.017)​rad\phi_{\rm CP}=(-0.004\pm 0.055\pm 0.017)~\text{rad}, where the first and second uncertainties are statistical and systematic, respectively.
This further enables the extraction of the weak and strong phase differences between the PP- and DD-wave amplitudes: (ξD−ξP)=(−0.15±2.25±0.69)​rad(\xi_{D}-\xi_{P})=(-0.15\pm 2.25\pm 0.69)~\text{rad} and (δD−δP)=(−0.97±0.88±0.34)​rad(\delta_{D}-\delta_{P})=(-0.97\pm 0.88\pm 0.34)~\text{rad}. Additionally, the polarization correlations between Ω−\Omega^{-} and Ω¯+\bar{\Omega}^{+} are measured.

  

The Standard Model, despite its remarkable success, fails to explain the fundamental matter-antimatter asymmetry observed in the universe [1, 2]. This is one of cosmology’s greatest unsolved puzzles, and as elucidated by A.D. Sakharov, it requires three essential conditions for dynamically generating the asymmetry [3]. Among them is CP violation (CPV), the violation of the combined charge-conjugation and parity symmetry. [5, 4].
Although the Standard Model predicts small CPV [6, 7] and it has been well established in meson decays [8, 9, 10], the effects observed to date, including the first observation in baryon decays by LHCb with 5.2σ\sigma significance [11], are insufficient to account for the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe [3, 4, 12]. Therefore, searches for new sources of CPV remain important. Investigating CPV in higher-spin, multi-strange baryons such as the Ω−\Omega^{-} baryon, provides a unique perspective for CPV test since it is the only observed spin-3/2 baryon that decays purely via the weak interaction. Nevertheless, experimental studies of the Ω\Omega baryon remain scarce.

In the weak hyperon decays,
CP symmetry is tested by comparing the angular distributions of a decay and its charge-conjugate process, with CPV asymmetries arising from interference among the contributing decay amplitudes. In the Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda process, the amplitude is described by a parity-conserving (PP-wave) and a parity-violating (DD-wave)
amplitude, quantified in terms of the decay parameters α\alpha, β\beta, and γ\gamma [13, 14, 15]. These parameters are constrained by the normalization condition:

α2+β2+γ2=1,\displaystyle\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=1,

(1)

where β\beta and γ\gamma are expressible in terms of α\alpha and the relative phase ϕ\phi between the parity-conserving and parity-violating amplitudes:

β=1−α2sinϕ,γ=1−α2cosϕ,\displaystyle\beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\sin\phi,\quad\gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\cos\phi,

(2)

while the antiparticle has the corresponding decay parameters α¯\bar{\alpha} and ϕ¯\bar{\phi}. To construct tests of CP symmetries, we use the following CP-violation observables [16, 17] to compare the particle and antiparticle decay parameters:

ACP=α+α¯α−α¯,BCP=β+β¯α−α¯,ϕCP=ϕ+ϕ¯2.A_{\rm CP}=\frac{\alpha+\bar{\alpha}}{\alpha-\bar{\alpha}},\quad B_{\rm CP}=\frac{\beta+\bar{\beta}}{\alpha-\bar{\alpha}},\quad\phi_{\rm CP}=\frac{\phi+\bar{\phi}}{2}.

(3)

The Ω−\Omega^{-} baryon predominantly decays through the Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda and Ω−→Ξ​π\Omega^{-}\to\Xi\pi channels (Ξ0​π−\Xi^{0}\pi^{-} and Ξ−​π0\Xi^{-}\pi^{0})[18], all of which receive contributions from both PP-wave and DD-wave amplitudes. A non-vanishing direct CP-violating asymmetry in these decays requires two essential conditions: (1) the interference between different partial waves (e.g., PP-DD wave interference within a given decay channel); and (2) the coexistence of both CP-even and CP-odd amplitudes that acquire non-zero relative phase differences. This mechanism is analogous to that established in K→π​πK\to\pi\pi decays [8], where CPV requires both a weak phase (from the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix) and a strong phase (from final-state interactions) [7, 19]. Therefore, the CP asymmetries ACPA_{\rm CP} and BCPB_{\rm CP} must incorporate both the strong-phase difference (δD−δP\delta_{D}-\delta_{P}) and the weak-phase difference (ξD−ξP\xi_{D}-\xi_{P}) between partial waves in each decay channel [20, 21, 22]. Actually, a full description of the decay amplitudes would require the inclusion of channel-coupling effects [20]. According to the theoretical model in Ref. [20], the CP-violating asymmetry in the Ω−→Ξ​π\Omega^{-}\to\Xi\pi decay channel is predicted to be very small. This is primarily because the weak-phase difference in the Ω−→Ξ​π\Omega^{-}\to\Xi\pi channel is calculated to be much smaller than in the Ω−→Λ​K−\Omega^{-}\to\Lambda K^{-} channel, and its strong-phase difference also leads to a suppressed CP-violating effect. Consequently, the Ω−→Ξ​π\Omega^{-}\to\Xi\pi channel has a negligible impact on the extraction of (ξD−ξP\xi_{D}-\xi_{P}) and (δD−δP\delta_{D}-\delta_{P}) at leading order. This justifies an approach where these parameters are estimated using only the decay parameters from Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda decays [20]:

tan(δD−δP)≈β−β¯α−α¯,tan(ξD−ξP)≈β+β¯α−α¯.\displaystyle\tan(\delta_{D}-\delta_{P})\approx\frac{\beta-\bar{\beta}}{\alpha-\bar{\alpha}},\quad\tan(\xi_{D}-\xi_{P})\approx\frac{\beta+\bar{\beta}}{\alpha-\bar{\alpha}}.

(4)

All CP asymmetries defined above depend on the parameters αΩ−\alpha_{\Omega^{-}}, αΩ¯+\alpha_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}}, ϕΩ−\phi_{\Omega^{-}}, and ϕΩ¯+\phi_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}}. Previous measurements determined αΩ−=0.0154±0.002\alpha_{\Omega^{-}}=0.0154\pm 0.002 and αΩ¯+=−0.018±0.004\alpha_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}}=-0.018\pm 0.004 with high precision [23, 24, 25]. The angle ϕΩ\phi_{\Omega} remained unmeasured until the BESIII study [26], although that study did not account for CP violation in Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda.

Beyond CP asymmetries, the spin-entangled production of Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} pairs in e+​e−→ψ⁡(3686)→Ω−​Ω¯+e^{+}e^{-}\to\psi(3686)\to\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} provides a unique opportunity to study polarization correlations between a spin-3/2 baryon and its antibaryon. The polarization of a single Ω−\Omega^{-} has been studied via the decay chain Ω−→K−​Λ,Λ→p​π−\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda,\Lambda\to p\pi^{-} [27, 28, 29]. Unlike spin-1/2 particles with a spin vector and two form factors [32, 33, 34, 35, 30, 31], spin-3/2 systems provide richer polarization information, including a quadrupole and an octupole tensor (fifteen independent components) parameterized by three helicity amplitude ratios hih_{i} and ϕi\phi_{i} (i=1,3,4i=1,3,4) [27, 28, 36, 37]. However, the correlations between Ω−\Omega^{-} and Ω¯+\bar{\Omega}^{+} in such an entangled state have never been measured. Simultaneous reconstruction of the pair should reveal significant polarization correlations between the two particles [28].
To address this, we adopt the approach previously employed in other hyperon decay analyses [38], but now applied to the CP estimation in the process e+​e−→ψ⁡(3686)→Ω−​Ω¯+e^{+}e^{-}\to\psi(3686)\to\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+}, allowing for a simultaneous description of all decay sequences.

In this Letter, using (2.71±0.01)×109(2.71\pm 0.01)\times 10^{9} ψ⁡(3686)\psi(3686) events from the BESIII detector [39, 40], we derive the joint angular distribution for the spin-3/2 Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} entanglement from ψ⁡(3686)\psi(3686) decays. Reconstructing the decays Ω−→K−Λ(→pπ−)\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda(\to p\pi^{-}) and Ω¯+→K+Λ¯(→p¯π+)\bar{\Omega}^{+}\to K^{+}\bar{\Lambda}(\to\bar{p}\pi^{+}) yields about 3300 events, enabling the first independent measurement of ϕΩ−\phi_{\Omega^{-}} and ϕΩ¯+\phi_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}}. Using the joint angular distribution of ξ=(θΩ−,θΛ,ϕΛ,θp,ϕp,θΛ¯,ϕΛ¯,θp¯,ϕp¯)\xi=(\theta_{\Omega^{-}},\theta_{\Lambda},\phi_{\Lambda},\theta_{p},\phi_{p},\theta_{\bar{\Lambda}},\phi_{\bar{\Lambda}},\theta_{\bar{p}},\phi_{\bar{p}}) (see Fig. 1) [29, 27, 26], we probe CP symmetry and the weak and strong phase differences in Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda and Ω¯+→K+​Λ¯\bar{\Omega}^{+}\to K^{+}\bar{\Lambda} decay, and present the Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} polarization correlations. Λ\Lambda momentum direction. The polar axis direction in the Λ\Lambda rest frame is z^Λ\widehat{z}_{\Lambda} and y^Λ\widehat{y}_{\Lambda} is along z^Ω−×z^Λ\widehat{z}_{\Omega^{-}}\times\widehat{z}_{\Lambda}. The decay of the Ω¯+\bar{\Omega}^{+} follows the same principle.

To ensure maximal signal purity, a double-tag method is employed by reconstructing both Ω−\Omega^{-} and Ω¯+\bar{\Omega}^{+} via their respective decay chains. To improve the detection efficiency, we allow also one π+\pi^{+} or π−\pi^{-} to be missing per event.
All charged tracks reconstructed from multilayer drift chamber hits are required to be within a polar-angle (θ\theta) range of |cos⁡θ|<0.93|\cos\theta|<0.93.
We require between five and seven such tracks.
To determine the species of final-state particles, specific energy loss (dEE/dxx) and time-of-flight system (TOF) information are used to form particle identification (PID) probabilities for the reconstructed pion, kaon, and proton hypotheses. Charged particles are identified as the hypothesis with the highest probability, and only one K−​(K+)K^{-}(K^{+}) and one proton (antiproton) are
required in each event. The remaining charged tracks in an event are assumed to be pions.
Vertex fits are performed by looping over all combinations with oppositely charged proton and pion candidates, constraining them to a common vertex. If there is more than one p​π−p\pi^{-} or p¯​π+\bar{p}\pi^{+} pair, the pair with an invariant mass closest to the nominal Λ\Lambda mass [41] is selected.
Given the possibility of an undetected π+\pi^{+} or π−\pi^{-} in the decay,
events containing at least one surviving Λ\Lambda or Λ¯\bar{\Lambda} candidate are retained.
In cases where both the π+\pi^{+} and π−\pi^{-} are found, one will be dropped and treated as missing, as discussed next, in order to obtain uniform mass resolution.
To further suppress background and improve the kinematic resolution, a one-constraint (1C) kinematic fit is performed under the p​p¯​K+​K−​π​πmissp\bar{p}K^{+}K^{-}\pi\pi_{\rm miss} hypothesis, treating one pion as a missing particle. The mass of this πmiss\pi_{\rm miss} is constrained to its Particle Data Group (PDG) value [41].
For events with both Λ\Lambda and Λ¯\bar{\Lambda} fully reconstructed, the pion assigned as “missing” is chosen from the pair forming the Λ\Lambda or Λ¯\bar{\Lambda} candidate that has the smallest χ2\chi^{2} from its vertex fit. Candidate events are required to satisfy χ1​C2\chi^{2}_{1\rm C} << 100.
In the following,
the πmiss\pi_{\rm miss} momentum is that obtained from the 1C kinematic fit and is used in invariant-mass calculations.

The selection criteria require the invariant masses Mp​πM_{p\pi}, Mp​πmissM_{p\pi_{\rm miss}}, MK​p​πM_{Kp\pi}, and MK​p​πmissM_{Kp\pi_{\rm miss}} to lie within [1.110, 1.122] GeV/c2c^{2}, [1.100, 1.132] GeV/c2c^{2}, [1.663, 1.681] GeV/c2c^{2}, and [1.655, 1.690] GeV/c2c^{2}, respectively.
Finally, the candidates for the angular distribution analysis are obtained by constraining the invariant masses of p​π−,p¯​π+p\pi^{-},\bar{p}\pi^{+} and p​π−​K−,p¯​π+​K+p\pi^{-}K^{-},\bar{p}\pi^{+}K^{+} to the PDG values of the Λ\Lambda and Ω\Omega particles, respectively.

The following systematic uncertainties are considered for the angular distribution measurement. The tracking and PID efficiencies for K±K^{\pm} and the reconstruction efficiencies for Λ,Λ¯\Lambda,\bar{\Lambda} are studied using the control samples of J/ψ→p​K−​Λ¯+c.c.J/\psi\to pK^{-}\bar{\Lambda}+\text{c.c.} and Λc+→Λ​X\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda X[43]. The efficiency dependencies are parameterized in two-dimensional space: as a function of (cos⁡θ\cos\theta, ptp_{\text{t}}) for kaons, and (cos⁡θ\cos\theta, pp) for Λ,Λ¯\Lambda,\bar{\Lambda}. The MC efficiency is then corrected by applying these two-dimensional efficiency scale factors. The associated systematic uncertainty is estimated by varying the scale factors by ±1​σ\pm 1\sigma independently within each corresponding two-dimensional bin. The differences between the new fit results and the nominal results are treated as systematic uncertainties. Systematic uncertainties from fixed parameters αΛ\alpha_{\Lambda} and αΛ¯\alpha_{\bar{\Lambda}} were estimated by varying their values by ±1​σ\pm 1\sigma and taking fitting result differences as uncertainties. The systematic uncertainty of the fit method is estimated from input-output checks using 200 MC samples, each with a size equivalent to the data sample, generated with the nominal fit parameters from the angular analysis [44]. The uncertainty is taken as the mean of the pull distribution scaled by the statistical uncertainty. The systematic uncertainties arising from the kinematic fits are examined by comparing the fitted decay parameters obtained with and without helix parameter corrections. Overall, this analysis provides a foundation for future hyperon CPV searches by demonstrating the power of full angular distribution measurements in Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} decays. We reconstruct spin-entangled Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} pairs to extract the complete angular distribution, yielding the CP-sensitive observables ϕΩ−\phi_{\Omega^{-}} and ϕΩ¯+\phi_{\bar{\Omega}^{+}}, as well as the full set of polarization correlations (including vector, tensor, and octupole components) between the two hyperons. This approach enables improved CPV measurements and provides access to Ω−\Omega^{-} polarization properties and their entanglement with Ω¯+\bar{\Omega}^{+}. Although the Ω−→Ξ​π\Omega^{-}\to\Xi\pi channel accounts for approximately 30% of Ω−\Omega^{-} decays and could offer complementary CPV signatures due to its distinct strong-phase behavior, we focus exclusively on the Ω−→K−​Λ\Omega^{-}\to K^{-}\Lambda channel in this initial analysis due to favorable statistics. Future studies incorporating multi-channel interference effects are expected to further enhance sensitivity. Notably, excluding the previously measured ACPA_{\rm CP}, all CP asymmetry observables and the Ω−​Ω¯+\Omega^{-}\bar{\Omega}^{+} polarization correlations presented in this work are measured for the first time.
